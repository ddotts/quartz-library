## Overview

Proto-realism was the empirical precursor to [[Realism]]. Its earliest cascade circuits preceded both the discovery of the aether plane and formal realist theory. Engineers influenced by [[Classicism|classical]] ritual analysis began treating spells as sequences whose smaller operations could be isolated, repeated, and triggered in order. They did not yet know that those operations could be described as evocation and transmutation, but their work created the foundation from which realism later emerged.

## First cascade circuits

[[Wyatt Tell]], an engineer at [[Vineflare]] in the [[Melissian Federation]], led the [[First Cascade Circuit Demonstration]] in AP 1448. The demonstration used linked low-level sigils to reproduce multiple effects of higher-order spells. Its circuits were inefficient, highly specialized, and difficult to repair, but trained crews could reproduce them. [[Potemkin 1]] incorporated descendants of these circuits, now known as [[Tell Circuits]], in its life-support, fabrication, and propulsion systems before its AP 1500 launch.

## First industrial revolution

This period is treated as magic's first industrial revolution. Magical labor became divisible and programmable, but remained tied to specific classical workings, proprietary notation, and large teams of specialists. The technology worked before its practitioners possessed a common theory of why it worked.
