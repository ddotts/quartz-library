## Common distribution

The Common distribution is the baseline population shared across settled space. It is a demographic pool rather than a separate people:

- Human: 25% of the Common distribution.
- Elf: 15%.
- Halfling: 15%.
- Tiefling: 15%.
- Dwarf: 15%.
- Orc: 10%.
- Other species: 5%.

The Other species share is always five percent of the local Common distribution, not five percent of the total local population. It allows any species to appear anywhere without creating another established demographic center. Local concentrations listed below sit outside the Common distribution. When Dwarves, Tieflings, or Orcs receive an additional local share, that share is added to their portion of the Common distribution.

No established non-common concentration exceeds ten percent. The Common distribution is always at least ten times the largest non-common concentration, ensuring that every individually named common species remains at least as prevalent as any locally concentrated non-common species.

## Legacy populations

### Lisden

Centaurs, Firbolgs, Harengon, Satyrs, Fairies, and Eladrin belong to [[Lisden]]'s pre-spaceflight history. They never constituted one fey civilization. Eladrin and Fairy dynasties ruled some of the kingdoms that competed for the planet, while Centaur, Firbolg, Harengon, and Satyr communities were prominent among the mixed kingdoms. Allegiance still crossed species lines. The victorious [[Fae Alliance]] incorporated Houses from several peoples, and their descendants remain attached to old settlements, territories, and Sylvan-speaking institutions under [[NOX ULTRA|NOX]].

### Lyore

Lyore's Aasimar arrived through celestial planar shifts before modern interstellar settlement. Their mobile tribes formed around sacred water towers, with the [[Valley of Gods]] becoming their most enduring center. They regard themselves as a legacy Lyorean people rather than an immigrant population, and later governments never replaced their kinship and ritual authorities.

### Broken Isles

Plasmoid and Thri-kreen communities entered Deresian history through the collapse that created the [[Broken Isles]]. They originated on different worlds, planes, and chronologies rather than in one shared homeland. Contact among fragments drew them into the languages and civic networks that became Deresian society, but fragment origin and Sea affiliation may remain more important than species identity.

## Migration histories

### Avergwyn and the populations that remained

Firbolg, Harengon, Leonin, Minotaur, Fairy, and Eladrin communities are old populations of [[Avergwyn]] that participated only lightly in the first expansion. Potemkin recruitment favored governments, technical institutions, and working communities able to supply large coordinated migrations. These peoples retained stronger ties to local lands, institutions, and kinship networks, so no comparable off-world demographic center formed. Their limited migration reflects historical recruitment and political choice rather than an inability to travel.

### The Schil–Sator–Cthel corridor

The strongest multi-species working-class migration began in [[Schil]]. Dwarves, Gnomes, Bugbears, Goblins, Hobgoblins, Goliaths, Dragonborn, and Kobolds joined the workers, families, associations, and firms that settled [[Sator]]. Goblin communities included migrants from the Veekz Islands, while other peoples came from Schil's towns and neighboring territories. Constructs entered the corridor through the shipyards and industrial institutions that increasingly recognized them as workers and residents rather than equipment.

Sator transformed these populations into a second Schil cultural center shaped by technical education, mutual aid, volcanic settlement, and the [[Rotator Stations]]. When [[Potemkin 1]] reached [[Cthel System|Cthel]], a second migration departed primarily from Sator rather than directly from Avergwyn. On [[Typhon]], the descendants of that diaspora developed Cthelic institutions and identities adapted to life inside the Hollow.

### The weaker Typhon–Tybalt flow

Migration from Typhon to [[Tybalt]] is a smaller reverse branch of the corridor rather than a fourth equal historical center. Dwarf, Gnome, Bugbear, Goblin, Hobgoblin, Goliath, Dragonborn, Kobold, and Construct families followed [[deGraf's]] contracts, independent workshops, and family connections into Firan. Tybalt offered surface settlements, open sky, and ordinary horizons despite its heavy gravity and magnetic instability. The present demographic concentration also includes direct Firan and corporate migration, so it does not measure the strength of the Typhon flow alone. Most arrivals entered existing Firan Creole communities instead of recreating Cthelic society.

### Quamridge Mass

Ratfolk, Tiefling, Tabaxi, Kobold, and Yuan-ti settlement preceded the modern racial order on [[Quamridge Mass]]. Competing corporations recruited entire communities, financing transport, housing, schools, and workshops in exchange for industrial settlement. Collective recruitment initially gave migrant associations bargaining power and helped create the neighborhoods and working culture from which Firan Creole emerged.

Segregation hardened only after a failed cross-species industrial uprising against unsafe work, polluted living levels, restricted gravity compensation, and growing corporate control. Authorities recast the uprising as proof that the migrant populations were dangerous. Emergency controls divided districts, restricted movement and employment, and created ancestry-based risk classifications. These measures outlived the crisis and became the foundation of Quamridge's racial hierarchy. Tieflings were eventually assigned its lowest general status, while limited access to the spires for cooperative elites prevented the order from becoming absolute segregation.

### Ping

Genasi, Orc, and Triton communities predate most modern dome settlement on [[Ping]]. Orc communities contributed the Old Orcish stratum preserved in Issiz, while Genasi traditions developed around the planet's reverberating elemental and magical conditions. Triton histories preserve a relationship with buried waters, deep reservoirs, saltwater caverns, and ancient cistern routes beneath the desert. Modern dome expansion divided these peoples between the Nila cities and mobile desert communities whose control of water, sacred places, and family law depends on remaining outside permanent dome authority.

### Nereid

Lizardfolk, Tortle, and Triton migrants formed the first large communities able to live beyond [[Nereid]]'s artificial surface islands. Tritons established deep settlements, Tortle communities organized around mobile islands and seasonal circuits, and Lizardfolk settlements bridged shallow-water infrastructure with surface platforms. Successive crews sponsored relatives and constructed permanent habitats. Their communities remain because Nereid permits aquatic civic life independent of surface governments, although extraction concessions continue to contest control of that space.

### Lyore's frontier migration

Centaurs, Giff, Leonin, Loxodons, Minotaurs, Owlin, and Satyrs reached [[Lyore]] through successive waves of mining, hauling, mercenary work, and NOX settlement. The [[Nova Independence War]] attracted people seeking distance from established governments, while independence and the AC 961 consolidation encouraged families to leave ships for Quexez, Valgrath, and the mining settlements. Their descendants usually organize through regional, occupational, Orda, or NOX institutions rather than species colonies.

### The Broken Isles' open-space migration

Giff, Hadozee, Loxodon, Goliath, and Hobgoblin migrants arrived after the [[Broken Isles]] joined the wider transport network. Breathable open space, three-dimensional settlements, armed work, and fragmented jurisdiction allowed crews, military units, refugee flotillas, and transport cooperatives to become permanent communities. Their descendants are divided among Confederation institutions, the concessionary order, local defense movements, and the [[Alliance of Worlds]].

### Eden

Aarakocra, Kenku, and Owlin migrated to [[Eden]] during the expansion of the [[Universal Federation]]. Many came from stations and hazardous settlements built without their communities in mind. Eden's open skies, stable environment, education, and Federal citizenship supported permanent settlements. Their shared political networks developed on Eden, but they preserve separate origins and do not constitute one avian culture.

### Procne

Procne's minority populations combine older survival communities with later industrial migration. Some Genasi, Lizardfolk, Tortle, and Yuan-ti communities descend from populations that lived beyond the stabilized eye before modern colonization, in storm belts, wetlands, reservoirs, and sheltered reaches. Genasi and Dragonborn also arrived through atmospheric harvesting, storm engineering, shipping, and later SCF institutions. The SCF's relocation after the [[Palade Cataclysm]] accelerated migration into Portas, while older populations remained outside the eye and retained claims predating corporate rule.

### Gas-giant habitats

Aarakocra communities around gas giants descend largely from migrants trained on Eden, Procne, and earlier stations. Atmospheric extraction drew the first crews; successful campaigns for larger flight spaces, open shafts, and less restrictive habitat architecture then attracted families. Communities around Oomi and in the [[Lagal System]] now form a station chain connected through marriages, apprenticeships, religious visits, and labor organizing rather than a single planetary homeland.

### Mobile diasporas

The mobile diasporas are the accumulated result of shipbuilding, war, trade, displacement, pilgrimage, and deliberate preference for station life. Hadozee communities grew around large habitats and ships; Kenku followed Coast, courier, and communications routes; Thri-kreen and Plasmoids left the Broken Isles through work and family networks; Tabaxi spread through trade, court service, and private contracting; Gnomes, Bugbears, and Goblins entered through industrial migrations; and Constructs moved with shipyards and fleets while pursuing recognition as residents rather than transferable equipment.

Ships and stations are permanent homes for these communities. Their political loyalties commonly attach to a vessel, guild, station, fleet, religion, or mobile nation rather than an ancestral planet.
