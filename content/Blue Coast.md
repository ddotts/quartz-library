## Overview

The Blue Coast is an FTL ring network stretching from [[Aldan System|Aldan]] to the edge of the known spiral. The network began at Aldan in AC 0 and was extended by three different vessels: Potemkin 2, its successor the Odyssey, and [[Potemkin 1]] at Core. It is therefore associated with the [[Tsuiseki]] without being entirely their creation. The nodes connect the systems visited by those vessels from [[Nearspace]] to the spiral edge; the Odyssey's move into the [[Nodal Trough]] in AC 979 is the first major leg of its pilgrimage without an established chain ahead of it.

## How the network works

The rings use technology similar to [[ATP Jump Drive|ATP jump drives]], scaled to create teleportation circles several kilometers wide. Each circle is anchored to joined asteroids large enough to carry the ley lines that sustain the ring and its shipyards. Blue Coast travel depends on compatible navigation hardware. [[Ruby Abyss]] holds the only commercial license for those packages, turning a [[Tsuiseki]]-built public route into one of the [[Collective Commercial Space|CCS]]'s most consequential technical dependencies. The Coast displaced generation ships as the normal means of expansion, but it did not eliminate conventional interstellar travel. [[Weiß]], the [[Haulers Guild]], independent couriers, and smugglers still maintain slower routes that can bypass the nodes.

## Aid and authority

Every major node supports a Hospitaller chapter-house and, through it, [[Red Feather]] logistics. The Coast is therefore simultaneously transport infrastructure, a [[Tsuiseki]] pilgrimage trail, and the backbone of galactic emergency aid. The most politically sensitive point in the network is not ownership of the rings but control of their approaches. Local governments police the surrounding space, [[Ruby Abyss]] supplies the licensed navigation layer, and the Tsuiseki claim authority over the nodes themselves; every transit therefore crosses three overlapping jurisdictions. Hospitaller chapter-houses keep independent arrival ledgers for rescue and epidemiological work. Those records are indispensable during disasters, but their existence also gives the Tsuiseki a uniquely complete picture of movement across the galaxy and makes the Coast's humanitarian neutrality a recurring political question.
