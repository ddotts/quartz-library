# Blue Coast

## Overview

The FTL ring network left behind by the [[Odyssey]] and Potemkin 2. Spans from [[Aldan System|Aldan]] to the edge of the known spiral. The network began at Aldan in AC 0 and was extended by three different vessels: Potemkin 2, its successor the Odyssey, and [[Potemkin 1]] at Core. It is therefore associated with the [[Tsuiseki]] without being entirely their creation. The nodes connect the systems visited by those vessels from [[Nearspace]] to the spiral edge; the Odyssey's move into the [[Nodal Trough]] in AC 979 is the first major leg of its pilgrimage without an established chain ahead of it.

The ring network utilises similar technology to [[ATP Jump Drive|ATP jump drives]], but scaled to produce a several-kilometre-wide Teleportation Circle, anchored on Asteroids hauled and attached together to create a sizeable enough astral body to contain ley lines to sustain the drain of the arcane power of the circle and the shipyards. Blue Coast travel depends on compatible navigation hardware. [[Ruby Abyss]] holds the only commercial license for those packages, turning a [[Tsuiseki]]-built public route into one of the [[Collective Commercial Space|CCS]]'s most consequential technical dependencies. The Coast displaced generation ships as the normal means of expansion, but it did not eliminate conventional interstellar travel. [[Weiß]], the [[Haulers Guild]], independent couriers, and smugglers still maintain slower routes that can bypass the nodes.

Every major node supports a Hospitaller chapter-house and, through it, [[Red Feather]] logistics. The Coast is therefore simultaneously transport infrastructure, a [[Tsuiseki]] pilgrimage trail, and the backbone of galactic emergency aid. The most politically sensitive point in the network is not ownership of the rings but control of their approaches. Local governments police the surrounding space, [[Ruby Abyss]] supplies the licensed navigation layer, and the Tsuiseki claim authority over the nodes themselves; every transit therefore crosses three overlapping jurisdictions. Hospitaller chapter-houses keep independent arrival ledgers for rescue and epidemiological work. Those records are indispensable during disasters, but their existence also gives the Tsuiseki a uniquely complete picture of movement across the galaxy and makes the Coast's humanitarian neutrality a recurring political question.
