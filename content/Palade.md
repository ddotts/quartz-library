- **Population:** No current residents; the demographic profile below describes Palade's final pre-cataclysm population.
- **Languages:** In the final pre-cataclysm population, [[Common Language|Common (Imperial)]] 92%; [[Deresian|Belter Deresian]] 28%; [[Common Language|Common (Milessian)]] 18%.
- **Beliefs:** In the final pre-cataclysm population, [[Tsuiseki|Followers of the Path]] 11%; [[Her Church of Light|Her Church of Light]] 8%; [[The Moiety]] 14%; local traditions 18%; atheism 49%.

## Overview

Palade was formerly a diverse-biome planet (similar to [[Avergwyn]] or [[Eden]]).

## Appearance

Palade still resembles an Earth-like blue-green world from a distance, but a persistent greenish haze blankets the planet and hides its abandoned surface.

## Imperial capital

[[Redmond Daeson|Lord Redmond Daeson]] was crowned here in AC 546, establishing it as the [[Imperium]]'s capital; the peace ending the [[First Galactic War]] was signed at [[Eden]], but the Imperium's seat was Palade. Served as [[Space Commerce Federation|SCF]] headquarters from AC 697 until the [[Palade Cataclysm]] (AC 799) rendered the planet uninhabitable; SCF relocated to [[Procne]]. Now completely abandoned.

## From SCF rule to catastrophe

Palade was the [[Imperium]]'s capital from [[Redmond Daeson|Daeson]]'s coronation in AC 546 and then the [[Space Commerce Federation|SCF]] headquarters until the AC 799 Cataclysm made the planet surface uninhabitable. The disaster remains both an arcane mystery and an unresolved wartime accusation: the SCF blamed the [[Universal Federation]], the Federation denied responsibility and withheld humanitarian support, and later investigations dropped charges for lack of evidence without establishing a cause. The Cataclysm shifted the corporate center of gravity to [[Procne]], directly shaping the later geography of the [[Collective Commercial Space|CCS]].

## An abandoned archive

The planet is abandoned, but its former status means surviving records or material evidence could alter the accepted history of the [[Corporate War]]. The [[Plane Shift Disaster]] at [[Dome Yüceil]] in AC 970 gives Palade a new comparative case. Both were arcane catastrophes with major political casualties, but only the later event has modern witnesses and an explicit comparison to the shifts of [[Kur]]; investigators who link the two risk reopening accusations the AC 855 settlement never resolved.
