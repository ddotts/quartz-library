Deresian originated in the [[Broken Isles]], where communities from more than fifty collapsed worlds and histories were forced into contact. It is a creole language family shaped by isolation, migration, and the need to reconcile incompatible vocabularies and chronologies. The [[Collapse of the Deres Observation Paradox]] normalized the passage of time but did not erase the linguistic differences it produced.

- **Islandic Deresian** is the broad family of local varieties spoken across the Broken Isles. Communities separated by unstable routes or different planar origins preserve vocabulary, grammar, and oral traditions unknown in neighboring fragments.
- **Bloc Deresian**, formerly called **Union Deresian**, is a co-official language of the [[Concessionary Bloc]]. It standardized shared mercantile and administrative elements through commerce and institutions such as [[Cofradia]]. Critics dispute whether the standard represents Deresian communities or the concessionary institutions that govern them.
- **High Deresian**, formerly called **Pilgrim Deresian**, developed through [[The Odyssey]], the [[Tsuiseki]], and their humanitarian mission in the Broken Isles. Its Dindessian influence reflects the languages carried by the early pilgrimage. It remains a language of Path worship, scholarship, diplomacy, and formal service along the [[Blue Coast]].
- **Belter Deresian** is the working creole of station crews and mobile labor. It mixes local Islandic forms, the administrative standard of Bloc Deresian, and the formal vocabulary of High Deresian. Its practical vocabulary spread through docks, extraction sites, maintenance crews, and shipping networks.

The varieties overlap rather than form clean borders. A station worker from the Isles may move among Islandic, Belter, Bloc, and High Deresian in the same day. This code-switching can signal intimacy, profession, faith, institutional authority, or resistance to official authority.
