## Origins

Applied magic is the engineering discipline associated with [[Realism]], but its history begins with the [[Proto-realism|proto-realist]] circuits used aboard [[Potemkin 1]]. Formal realism supplied the explanation and common vocabulary that early circuit builders lacked; it did not retroactively invent their craft.

## Standardization

[[Iria Solavel]], a systems architect at [[Sator Polytechnic]], converted the realist model into shared component notation and testing procedures. Her work culminated in the AP 1764 [[Standard Cascade Circuit]]. A high-level spell could thereafter be designed as a programmed chain of lower-level evocations and transmutations whose components were manufactured, inspected, and replaced independently.

## Second industrial revolution

This standardization drove magic's second industrial revolution. [[The Odyssey|Potemkin 2]] was designed during the transition and departed in AC 0 with mature cascade systems. By then industrial magic was no longer confined to singular state projects: its methods had settled into shipbuilding, infrastructure, manufacturing, and professional education. Applied techniques continued to improve after realism ceased to answer the newest theoretical questions.
