## Overview

Fringe and esoteric traditions sit outside the principal [[Magic's Schools of Thought|schools of magical thought]] for different reasons. Fringe theories propose explanations that have produced little useful research, while esoteric practices produce repeatable results without being fully explained or reproduced by academic and industrial magic.

## Mnemonics

Mnemonics is a fringe theory proposing that collective understanding and memory shape reality and therefore determine how magic works. Under this theory, a spell succeeds partly because people remember the world in a form that permits it. Languages, myths, education, and archives would then be magical infrastructure, not merely records of magical practice.

The theory is based on the fantasy works of [[Lorena de Ecria y Ellea]] rather than experimental research and has produced no deeper arcane or scientific understanding. It is not generally considered a productive subject of further study.

## Spirit and blood magic

Spirit magic and blood magic are esoteric traditions whose results cannot be consistently reproduced by academic or industrial systems. Unlike [[Applied Magic|applied magic]], which divides spells into standardized sequences of evocation and transmutation, these practices depend on methods that have not been isolated into programmable components. Their continued effectiveness suggests either that established theories are incomplete or that some magic depends on relationships and conditions that resist standardization.

These traditions are generally described as esoteric rather than primitive. Their methods may be older, but their continued advantage in specific domains is an active limit on claims that applied magic has achieved complete mastery.

## Avergwyn astral beliefs

Early Avergwyn astrology organized aspects around four constellations. Speur Uaine represented nature and animal instinct, Rionnag Dhearg the order and power of the world, Speur Gorm mortal ingenuity, and Rionnag Purpaidh society and human power. These traditions described people becoming avatars by embodying an aspect of their world. The phenomena they grouped together are now studied through [[Leylines]], planetary [[Anima|Animas]], and [[Ascended Godhood]].

Some practitioners treated the Illusory Astral Tarot as more than a way to represent the aspects. Its handout describes each card as an illusory proxy for a remote artifact, aligned to a thread of the weave. A sufficiently attuned bearer was said to be able to draw the artifact along a leyline. The surviving account leaves the operation of the deck uncertain. Togail Bhreugan's mask belongs to a related tradition and appears in historical accounts of [[El Pesar]].

## Ping desert traditions

Ping's desert traditions preserve accounts of planar gates, ancient patrons, and bodily transformation. Runic inscriptions at the Obsidian Tower describe the Maleables altering their bodies through rites involving obsidian, lava, and souls. The inscriptions describe Argentum as a way to restore firmness to an altered body.

In _The Sultans of Ping_, Caleb describes five planar gates that once led to different destinations, then opened onto Avernus before closing. He attributes some of the desert's lingering magic to that incursion and describes the Ancient Ones, the [[Fomori]], and the Grua as distinct powers to which the inhabitants turned. He calls the Grua native gods of Ping and links the Bone Matriarch's rites to Grua'knoith. These identifications come through Caleb's disputed account of desert traditions.
