## Overview

Fringe and esoteric traditions sit outside the principal [[Magic's Schools of Thought|schools of magical thought]] for different reasons. Fringe theories propose explanations that have produced little useful research, while esoteric practices produce repeatable results without being fully explained or reproduced by academic and industrial magic.

## Mnemonics

Mnemonics is a fringe theory proposing that collective understanding and memory shape reality and therefore determine how magic works. Under this theory, a spell succeeds partly because people remember the world in a form that permits it. Languages, myths, education, and archives would then be magical infrastructure, not merely records of magical practice.

The theory is based on the fantasy works of [[Lorena de Ecria y Ellea]] rather than experimental research and has produced no deeper arcane or scientific understanding. It is not generally considered a productive subject of further study.

## Spirit and blood magic

Spirit magic and blood magic are esoteric traditions whose results cannot be consistently reproduced by academic or industrial systems. Unlike [[Applied Magic|applied magic]], which divides spells into standardized sequences of evocation and transmutation, these practices depend on methods that have not been isolated into programmable components. Their continued effectiveness suggests either that established theories are incomplete or that some magic depends on relationships and conditions that resist standardization.

These traditions are generally described as esoteric rather than primitive. Their methods may be older, but their continued advantage in specific domains is an active limit on claims that applied magic has achieved complete mastery.
