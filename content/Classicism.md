## Overview

Classicism is the oldest of the principal [[Magic's Schools of Thought|schools of magical thought]] and the inherited framework familiar from traditional magical education. It divides magic into multiple schools, treats ritual as the actuator and reality as the medium, and assumes that distinct systems govern distinct aspects of the world. A classical practitioner learns the correct structure for a type of effect rather than reducing all effects to a common mechanism.

## Strengths and limitations

The strength of classicism is accumulated practice. Its categories preserve reliable spells and rituals even when their underlying mechanism is uncertain. Its weakness is explanatory fragmentation: if each school works by different rules, the framework struggles to explain why their methods can interact or be recombined. This limitation later motivated [[Realism]].

## Historical centers

Classicism was not invented by one scholar. The Avergwyn-based [[Melissian University of Arcane Practices]] and [[Dindessian Temple of knowledge]] are the earliest known centers of its study and research, both dating to around 500 AP.
