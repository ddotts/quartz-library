## Overview

Magic's schools of thought are competing theoretical models for how magic changes reality. They are distinct from the classic schools of magic, which classify spells by the kinds of effects they produce. Each school of thought instead attempts to explain what initiates a magical change, what carries that change, and which rules make it repeatable.

## Aether and theoretical change

The discovery and theorisation of the aether plane, and to a lesser extent the void plane, transformed these explanations. The aether was described as a plane of pure potential and offered an explanation for where the energy of magical change originated. It did not explain how a practitioner could interface with reality, leaving room for several incompatible answers.

## Actuator, medium, and rules

Modern magical theory distinguishes among the **actuator**, the means by which change is initiated; the **medium**, what carries or constitutes that change; and the **rules**, the model used to make the result repeatable. The schools disagree because they assign these roles differently. Their arguments concern not only technique but what reality is and why it can be altered.

## Principal schools

- **[[Classicism]]:** inherited ritual systems govern distinct aspects of reality.
- **[[Realism]]:** responded to classicism by reducing magic to evocation and transmutation of aether.
- **[[Rationalism]]:** responded to realism by making understanding, rather than ritual, the means of shaping aether.
- **[[Impressionism]]:** brought rationalist ideas, developed through psionics, back to magic and treated reality as readable and changeable from within.
- **[[Romanticism]]:** applied impressionism back to classicism, preserving ritual systems as models for addressing a mutable reality.
- **[[Expressionism]]:** rejected romanticism's rigid systems in favor of personal rituals assembled from intertwined forms of magic.
- **[[Surrealism]]:** radicalized expressionism by treating every magical method as another mutable part of reality.

The history preceding formal realism is described under [[Proto-realism]], while theories and practices outside the principal sequence are collected in [[Fringe and Esoteric Traditions]].

## Applied disciplines

[[Applied Magic]] and [[Psionics]] are practical disciplines derived from realism and rationalism respectively, rather than separate schools of thought. They are described separately because applied magic enabled magical industrialization, while psionics carried rationalist ideas into the development that produced impressionism.
